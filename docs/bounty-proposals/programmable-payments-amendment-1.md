# Bounty 01 design amendment for review · November 23, 2026

Please review a change to the design on #34. The November 20 proposal lets an owner write a spending guard and configure how a royalty is calculated. Its private prototype still has separate Python paths for standing orders, grants and escrow settlements. A new combination can therefore require a node change. I propose one rule interface through which owners can combine the supported payment actions. A merchant could start with a royalty on settlement, then install a second policy that divides the royalty between two payees and waits for proof of delivery. [Original proposal](https://github.com/IDNI/tau-testnet/issues/34#issuecomment-7025706101) · [Maintainer’s acceptance criterion](https://github.com/IDNI/tau-testnet/pull/146#pullrequestreview-5995504067)

The earlier prototype and its signed blocks predate approval. They exercise the original design. The rule interface and the acceptance runs below are proposals. Please confirm that this design and the disclosed prototype timing are acceptable before upstream implementation proceeds.

## What a policy author signs

The wallet would publish a versioned document containing a bounded set of Tau rules, a typed input/output declaration and a list of permitted effects. The owner signs the document hash, network, version, source of funds, allowed destinations, fee terms and upper bounds. A policy can name only its own balance or an escrow that explicitly authorizes the policy. Destination slots resolve to signed keys or to a named party in a verified record whose authority the owner accepted at installation. A submitter can supply an occurrence ID, an event ID or a grantee-signed draw request. None of these gives the submitter authority to choose an owner's debit or rewrite a payee.

| The policy can read | Who supplies it |
| --- | --- |
| Amounts, keys, basis points, calendar specification and caps fixed at installation | The owner's signed document |
| Candidate UTC time, eligible calendar occurrence, period, prior spend, unused escrow, last use and consumed markers | The node, from the candidate block and its projected state after earlier transactions |
| Gross amount and credited account of a confirmed payment, or the verified signer and subject of an attestation | A typed record identified by chain, transaction and event index. A tagged sale comes from an accepted buyer and an actual credit. A delivery statement binds the designated attestor, policy version and sale or escrow ID. |
| Requested amount and permissible destination of a grant draw | Bob's signed request, checked against the named grant, his key and his current sequence |

Wallets can compile an editor's formulas to a restricted, deterministic Tau fragment or submit the same fragment directly. Its public vocabulary would cover typed comparisons, Boolean combinations, checked unsigned arithmetic and floor division by a positive signed constant. Every output is assigned exactly once as a total expression over this invocation's inputs. Fix widths at installation, reject possible overflow unless the formula explicitly guards it and round fractions down before narrowing to transfer units. Reject undeclared streams, recursion, history references and unconstrained outputs at installation. Published source, input/output assignments and static bounds form part of the signed document. Authenticated facts must stay runtime inputs. Compiling the first observed sale amount into a policy would give the wrong royalty on the second sale.

For example, bind a confirmed gross to an input, the delivery attestation to another and two payee slots to signed keys. The author specifies “if the sale and delivery refer to the same event, pay slot A floor(gross × 3 / 100) and slot B floor(gross × 2 / 100), otherwise allow neither payment.” The event ID match and signer checks belong to the fact binding. The percentages and the choice to require delivery belong to the rule. No global stream numbers are reserved by this example.

## What validators execute

For each invocation the node binds the facts, evaluates the native Tau rule and reads a verdict and a bounded vector of effects. The common effects are paying a leg, reserving or releasing named escrow principal, charging a scoped meter and consuming a one-use event or occurrence. Each output position names one effect, source and destination binding at installation. The rule can enable a position and calculate its amount. Zero suppresses a leg. The node enforces signatures, source authority, allowed credit classes, limits, balance conservation and checked widths independently of the rule. An owner cannot omit a mandatory spend check, use a negative leg or consume another policy's event. The signed spending limits apply to the aggregate before settlement, even if several legs resolve to the same key. Every successful debit charges all applicable committed meters. Receipts record the policy hash/version, trigger ID, gross, legs, fees, meter changes and any refund at the canonical block.

Calendar occurrence, incoming credit, signed request, authenticated event and due deadline are the proposed trigger types. An overdue monthly occurrence lapses at the end of its signed window. For rent due on the 31st, February uses its last calendar day. Creation reserves no funds for rent. A failed payment leaves its occurrence open until the deadline. A cancellation takes effect in canonical transaction order. The node invokes the recipient's signed route on each covered original credit, including a direct transfer while the recipient is offline. Routing reads the gross once and does not count against the recipient’s outgoing spend. Derived credits are terminal. Refunds, fees, rewards and genesis or faucet credits are excluded. A route to the same account or an invalid destination fails at installation. A new retractable park checks both recipients’ applicable hold caps and commits its split at funding. Release uses that frozen split. Returning principal to the original owner bypasses routing.

A keeper signs with its own sequence and fee cap. The owner pays for installation, updates and any pre-funded expiry settlement. Execution quotes the keeper's fee from the actual principal legs under the existing fee rules. A route imposes no second transfer fee. If owner and keeper coincide, aggregate their debits before testing funds. Check the current sender and recipient policies on their applicable gross or derived leg. Count each economic payer's principal once toward spend and cooldown. Re-evaluate admission against canonical order at block application. A failed final leg leaves all balances, escrow, meters, markers, fees and accepted sequences as they were. Local worker timeouts indicate failure to validate locally and cannot define a different on-chain verdict.

## Fixtures for one node build

Once all five capabilities are activated on a private testnet, publish the node build digest, policy documents, signed inputs, genesis and expected receipts. Load both examples below onto that same build. The only difference between a straight royalty and an attested two-way royalty should be the signed rules and configuration. A reviewer should be able to install a third combination without editing the node.

* **Stage 1 · rent, routes and royalties.** Pay rent of 101 once per calendar month. The recipient's independent 20% savings route credits 81 and 20, rounding down. Run two keepers against the same occurrence. Only one succeeds. Give the savings account its own route and check that the routed 20 stops there. On the same build, install a straight 5% royalty and an attested 3% + 2% split on separate sale records for 1,000 units each. The first pays 50. A sale of 101 pays 5 once, even if two keepers submit the event. The second pays 30 and 20 only after the named attestor confirms that sale. A signature for a different sale fails. Reuse the existing policy with a second gross of 2,000 to obtain 60 and 40. The keeper cannot supply the gross.
* **Stage 2 · spending limits.** Compare two grants to Bob, one capped at 300 per draw and both referencing one 500-per-week allowance. A 300 draw from the first plus 200 from the second exhausts that allowance. Then try two 300-unit draws against a fresh 500-unit allowance. Only the first in block order succeeds. Only accepted draws charge the meter. Weekly periods start Monday at 00:00 UTC. Calendar months start on the first. Custom periods bind a duration and epoch.
* **Stage 3 · time restrictions.** Add an absolute opening, a 48-hour hold from funding and weekday business hours to the same draw policy. Both opening conditions must hold. Exercise one second before opening, the exact opening, the last eligible second and the exclusive close. Use validated block time. An expired window does not accumulate an entitlement.
* **Stage 4 · refunds.** Select the earliest configured absolute expiry, maximum lifetime or idle deadline. A successful draw resets idle time. A refused draw does not. The first eligible block settles due refunds in deadline and ID order, including when no keeper submits a transaction. An offline network settles on its next eligible block. Check that its stored balance and the returned amount sum to the original escrow less accepted draws.
* **Stage 5 · combined conditions.** Fund a single grant with 5,000, open it December 1, 2026, cap draws at 500 per Monday-based week and close it at the beginning of December 14. Here the same clock, meter, escrow and one-use bindings implement the grant with all three restrictions. Under the fixture's fee schedule, charge 10 for each accepted funding or draw and settle the refund from the funding fee. Bob pays the draw fees from his ordinary balance.

| UTC block and attempted action | Change in grant escrow | Principal received at this step | Fee charged |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Nov 30 · Alice funds the grant and Bob attempts an early draw | +5,000 | None | Alice 10 |
| Dec 1 · Bob draws 300 and 200, then requests one more unit | −500 | Vendors 500 | Bob 20 |
| Dec 7 · Bob draws 500 in the new week | −500 | Vendors 500 | Bob 10 |
| Dec 14 at 00:00 · Bob attempts a draw and the balance returns | −4,000 | Alice 4,000 | 0 |

The closing escrow balance is zero. Vendors receive 1,000. Alice recovers 4,000. Fees total 40 and are accounted for separately. Repeat with a failed recipient check on the last leg, two keepers, two draws in the same block, a stale policy version and a sibling block just before expiry. An independent node must derive the same balances, sequences, policy root and receipts after importing the signed blocks, restarting and switching branches.

## Deployment and limits

Governance would activate each stage after its prerequisite merges. Pre-activation blocks retain their historical state hashes. Old accounts need no migration. The private prototype's books are test fixtures. Public adoption starts with an empty, versioned registry at an agreed height. If any network already has live payment records by then, submit a separate migration with source-to-target roots, preservation of liabilities and replay vectors before activating there. Bind every authorization to its rule-engine version and prior policy hash. A future version cannot reinterpret an existing signed authorization.

The proposed upper bounds start at 8 KiB and 256 expression nodes per document, eight payee slots, 16 pending events per policy, 16 installed policies per owner per collection and 4,096 active entries per collection. Charge deterministic work units for input bindings, expression evaluation, effects, recipient checks and the due sweep. An idle chain can cross several deadlines between blocks. Reserve sufficient block capacity for every outstanding mandatory refund to fall due together, including indexed reads and receipt writes. Reject a new obligation that would exhaust that reserve. For this version the mandatory fallback releases remaining principal to the recorded funder, without depending on a fresh external input, fee balance or discretionary rule. Before activation, measure worst-case native execution, hashing, snapshot restoration and durable writes at those bounds, then publish the governed budget and capability response. Wallets need the active version and stages, accepted trigger and effect types, schedule and fee semantics, ordered preflight result and the queried canonical head.

For #33, every path that installs, weakens or removes protected terms must consult the previous committed guard, including rule offers and resets. An endorsement binds the network, account, prior and replacement terms, sequence, action and expiry. A delegate permitted to install exact terms cannot authorize a debit. Please identify the agreed #33 revision for the dependent stages and the recipient-controls revision for the stage 1 credit path. Development of the upstream auto-split and recipient-controls changes would be scheduled separately. I propose submitting each implementation and its user and operator documentation after approval and the preceding merge, with any further design changes returned for review.
