## Bounty 01 — Programmable payments

Please review this five-stage design for programmable payments under #34, #35 and #63. It targets `tau-testnet` at `c2b8c47`. The central question is what an owner can authorize once, while leaving anyone free to submit the eventual transaction. A keeper should be able to pay the rent while the owner is offline. The keeper chooses when to submit within the signed window. The owner sets the amount and destination in advance. [Five-stage proposal](https://github.com/947822urhbfhu/tau-testnet/blob/9a6f77615055aba609fff2d433c2f692e5dbeeea/docs/bounty-proposals/programmable-payments.md)

**Prototype and rollout.** A private prototype covered all five stages before design approval and before their prerequisites merged. Auto-split work also overlapped with recipient controls, which the program requires to be built separately. The program awards one builder $9,460 after all five stages merge. The proposed upstream review would start with stage 1, open each dependent stage after its prerequisite merges, and submit changes to an approved design as amendments. [Bounty terms](https://tau.net/bounty-program)

### Stage 1 · Payments the network starts · $3,380

**Standing payments.** The owner signs the recipient, amount, first due time, daily/weekly/monthly cadence, execution window and end condition. The end can be a date, payment count, total amount or explicit permission to continue until cancelled. Creation reserves no funds. A wallet timer would stop when the wallet closes, and handing a service the spending key would give it more authority than the rent requires. Here a payee, validator or other keeper submits one occurrence using its own key and sequence. Validators decide whether that occurrence is due.

Monthly rent due on the 31st falls on the last day of a shorter month and returns to the 31st when possible. Each UTC execution window ends at the earliest of its signed end, the next occurrence or the authorization’s expiry. An expired window lapses. Three missed months cannot become one surprise debit. Insufficient funds or a current policy refusal leave the occurrence available until its window closes. Two keepers racing to pay the same month can settle it once. A cancellation racing an execution follows canonical block order.

The signed envelope binds the genesis hash, sequence, action, deadlines and fee limit. The creation ID commits to the original terms. Changes identify the previous version and terms digest, retain past executions and preserve cumulative spend. Changing cadence requires a new authorization. Execution checks the owner’s current outgoing rule against the real recipient and amount. Where that rule requires co-approval, an endorsement binds the specific action, chain, occurrence, amount, recipient and expiry. Protected administrative changes also satisfy the previous guard. An endorsement to change terms grants no authority to execute a payment.

**Receive routes.** A recipient signs a destination, a share of 1–9,000 basis points, and optional minimum gross, start and expiry. A 20% route splits 101 units into 81 for the original payee and 20 for savings. Integer floor division leaves rounding dust with the original payee. The split is atomic across ordinary, approval-completed, standing and calculated payments. The original recipient’s incoming policy sees the gross. The savings destination’s policy sees its share and the routing account as payer. A refusal declines the whole payment. Derived credits terminate the route, so two accounts routing to each other cannot generate a loop.

For a retractable payment, check the recipients’ applicable hold caps and freeze the split when the funds are parked. Retraction before maturity returns the full principal to the original payer. Maturity pays the frozen amounts even if a recipient has since changed policy. Recomputing the split at release could redirect funds after the sender’s right to recall them had ended. A bound route has a signed expiry and cannot be removed unilaterally during its lock. [Receive-routing requirements](https://github.com/IDNI/tau-testnet/issues/35)

**Rule-computed payouts.** The owner signs up to eight payees, a typed amount expression per payee, and caps per execution, period and lifetime. Expressions support a confirmed gross amount, fixed amounts, addition and fractional multiplication with floor rounding. Check unsigned 64-bit intermediate bounds and the final 24-bit transfer amounts. Native Tau calculates each payee’s amount. A zero output omits that leg.

For a royalty, an authorized buyer signs a sale naming the rule. Crediting the merchant and recording the gross happen together. The keeper names that event. Accepting a keeper-supplied price would let the claimant set its own royalty. For escrow, the owner funds a settlement and names an attestor. A valid signature from that attestor authorizes release. The chain establishes who attested to delivery. Verification of the delivery itself belongs to the attestor. The event is consumed only when all legs settle. Unallocated or expired escrow returns to its owner. [Calculated-payout requirements](https://github.com/IDNI/tau-testnet/issues/63)

**Fees and spend.** The owner pays setup and update fees. The keeper pays the execution fee quoted from `o8` and `o9` on the principal legs. Check the combined requirement if one account has both roles. A routed share incurs no second ordinary fee. A successful economic debit counts once toward daily and weekly spend and cooldown. An optional isolated `o5` clause reads prior UTC-day spend, prior Monday-based week spend, the entire candidate amount and seconds since the previous accepted debit. The node supplies these four 64-bit inputs, including earlier debits in the same block. The ordinary parent-block balance input alone cannot distinguish two draws competing for the same allowance. This proposes scoped support for the accounting requested by #34 while #31 remains open.

### Stages 2–5 · Conditions attached to funds

**2. Spending limits · $1,420.** Alice funds an identified escrow grant for Bob. Bob signs draws from that grant to a destination and pays the draw fee from his unrestricted balance. Check Alice’s policy at funding, Bob’s outgoing policy at draw, and the destination’s incoming policy. If Bob has declared a recipient hold cap, he must also sign the exact restricted funding terms. Grant funds never enter his ordinary balance, where replacing his wallet policy could otherwise remove Alice’s restrictions.

Each draw must fit the remaining principal, any per-payment and per-grant cap, and any shared allowance for this grantor and grantee. With 4,600 units left, 400 drawn against a weekly grant cap of 900 and the same 400 counted against a shared weekly allowance of 500, Bob can draw **100**. Days and calendar months begin at 00:00 UTC and weeks on Monday. A fixed period signs its duration and epoch. Update each meter on a successful draw. Keep a shared allowance while funded grants reference it. [Wallet walkthrough and observed estimates](https://github.com/947822urhbfhu/tau-testnet/blob/9a6f77615055aba609fff2d433c2f692e5dbeeea/docs/bounty-proposals/reading-a-conditional-grant.md)

**3. Time restrictions · $1,380.** Use the validated block-header timestamp so independent nodes can replay the decision. The opening time is the later of an absolute start and funding time plus a relative hold. Weekday masks and half-open UTC time bands express working hours or an overnight window. Reject a grant with no usable time before expiry. A wallet estimate is advisory. In the walkthrough, an estimate for 300 passed. Another 400-unit draw then consumed Bob’s sequence. The old signature failed, a newly signed 101 exceeded the shared cap, and 100 passed preflight.

**4. Refunds · $1,520.** Alice may sign an absolute expiry, a duration from funding or a maximum idle period since funding or the last successful draw. The earliest deadline wins. At the boundary, refuse the draw and return remaining principal to Alice in the first eligible block’s post-transaction sweep. Process due grants by deadline and ID. The funding fee covers settlement. Returning principal triggers neither routing nor a second fee. The miner can wake for a due expiry on an otherwise idle private network.

**5. Combined conditions · $1,760.** Require an open time window and headroom under every applicable cap. Expiry takes precedence even if a fresh allowance period has started. The signed nine-block demonstration used a controlled clock, 10-unit fees on accepted fee-bearing transactions, and the following ledger.

Governance would activate each accepted stage. Earlier blocks keep their original state hashes. Wallets should show the current stage, last and next eligible occurrence, fee payer and receipts at a named canonical head. The walkthrough reads the grant, any shared allowance and the two accounts’ applicable rules at the same block. A head change between reads requires refreshing the set.

Proposed bounds include 4,096 active records per instruction collection, 16 per owner in each standing-order, grant, shared-allowance and payout book, 16 events per payout rule and 4,096 open events overall. A payout rule is limited to eight payees, 8 KiB and 256 expression nodes. Its evaluation also consumes the governed per-block work budget. A local in-memory sweep returned 172,032 units from 8,192 simultaneous expiries in 79.6–202.5 ms across nine runs. Account-history growth, whole-book hashing, durable writes and end-to-end block cost still need measurement before public activation.

Three decisions would settle the next step. First, which recipient-controls revision should stage 1 target? Second, what upstream #33 protection must land before conditional grants can proceed? The base prototype supports owner-plus-cosigner administration. A separate experiment lets a named delegate install one exact, previously authorized spend policy with its own fee while the owner is offline. General delegation and recovery remain open. Third, which network and activation stages should the public acceptance demo use? The signed blocks and genesis are available below. The wallet walkthrough describes the head and allowance checks for a grant draw. [Protected administration requirement](https://github.com/IDNI/tau-testnet/issues/33)
